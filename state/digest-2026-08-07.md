### The filter distribution — measured on real Qwant traffic (Roberto, `#proj-scale-serving-and-tracking` 8/05 19:57)

7,196 unique Qwant search terms expanded into **9,533 pCIV targets** — i.e. this is a direct measurement of what Varun's extraction produces.

| Filter | Targets | % |
|---|---:|---:|
| Categories | 8,044 | 84.4% |
| Brand | 5,362 | 56.3% |
| Condition | 973 | 10.2% |
| Age | 904 | 9.5% |
| Gender | 740 | 7.8% |
| Price target | 105 | 1.1% |
| Price min | 36 | 0.4% |
| Price max | 16 | 0.2% |

Most common combinations: Categories+Brand 3,979 (~42% of all targets), Categories only 2,600, Brand only 931, **no filters 505**, Categories+Gender+Age+Condition 346. Roberto: *"Requests with five or more filters are extremely rare. In fact, there was only one target in the entire dataset that contained all six filters."*

Two consequences. First, this retires the 8/05 concern that the feared six-filter case would block go-live — that case is one target in 9,533. Second, **the most expensive filter is also the rarest**: price appears on under 2% of targets while carrying the highest CPU and roughly doubling latency when combined. Roberto's own conclusion: *"The synthetic workload where every request applies all filters does not appear to be representative of production traffic."*

**Baseline for the latency budget.** Discover 2.x in prod: ~130–140 RPS, Vespa P95 125–230ms, CPU ~6% (while also handling ~1.2K feed requests/sec ingestion). Roberto's 3.0 test at 50 RPS: P95 ~125ms at 28% CPU — inside 2.x's prod latency envelope, though at far lower RPS and without concurrent ingestion.
