## Vespa result cache — a live finding that bears on Varun's numbers

`#proj-scale-serving-and-tracking` (C0B623CLQET) and `#vespa-optimizations-xfn` (C0BMYPZ2U8H) are not in the sweep's anchor list and both ran hot this morning. Saksham found that VSS has an in-memory result cache: key `sha256(serialized Vespa request JSON)`, 6000 entries, 5-minute TTL.

- **Dhaval, 09:14 today: "how is the cache applied in case of a structured civ request instead of just qt?"** Joseph answered "its the yql" at 09:17.
- **Saksham, 09:20:** because the key serializes the YQL, any filter change busts it. For the CIV path with filters applied, one filter checks price duration against `now()` at millisecond granularity, "So will basically not be caching the filter situation at all."
- **Saksham, 09:42:** "The `now()` problem would still be impacting us in prod since we'll have caching essentially disabled only for 3.0 as a result." He also questioned the standing "20-40 RPS in Prod" ceiling — if caching worked, it would be higher.
- **Bhupesh:** fewer than 1% of active PLAs have a sale price greater than zero with effective dates in the past, so the sale-price-effective-date filter can be dropped entirely. Ticket **AI-1619**.
- **Dhaval, 09:22:** "Great find … the data seems to back up that decision."
- **Joseph, 09:40:** the cache is also fixed-size (Discover 2 and 3 compete for it) and pod-local (pods do not mutually benefit).
- **Roberto Simoes and John Exantus** both confirmed the cache was disabled for the perf test, so the test numbers are cache-free.

Why it matters to Varun: the online CIV path sends a structured CIV request, and structured CIV plus filters is precisely the shape that never caches. Oren's "<250 ms from Europe" is conditioned on Vespa holding up.
