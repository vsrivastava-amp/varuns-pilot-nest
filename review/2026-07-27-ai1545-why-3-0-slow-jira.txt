Hey @Artem Dippel — went through the prod CSVs (cc @Saksham Bhatla). The slowdown has one specific shape: 3.0 is slower only when a brands filter is present AND the GPC value prefix-matches more than one category path. Every other shape runs within 2ms of 2.0 — including brand + single-path category (23.4ms vs 24.8). Multi-path GPC + brand: 110ms avg vs 23. Result count doesn't change it (0-ad rows cost the same as 100-ad rows), and the tail is clipped by the 150ms timeout — 46/1000 rows recorded no latency at all, so the published p95/p99 are floors.

The cause is the "X OR field-unset" filter construction. Unset brand is 0.96% of the corpus; the deployed approximate-threshold is 0.01. So a brand clause for any non-huge brand estimates as ultra-selective, Vespa abandons the ANN path, and evaluates the filter directly — which is expensive exactly when the category prefix expands to multiple paths (googleProductCategory has no rank:filter). Unset gender/age/condition are 8–29% of the corpus, which is why those filters never trigger this. [CONFIRMED via searchCoverage diagnostics on the PROD-TEST cluster — pending probe, see session notes.] Stage flips the same way, but direct evaluation over its small corpus is fast — that's why the slow slice reversed between stage (GPC-no-brand) and prod (GPC+brand).

The decision this points at: brand filters currently pay ~90ms extra on multi-path categories to include products with no brand set — 0.96% of the corpus. Is that trade intended? Dropping the "" sentinel from the brand clause — or moving unset-matching to a hasBrand bool with rank:filter — removes the whole regression. Exact IN for categories (vespaYqlVersionProduct=1) removes it too.

One flag for the latency-budget discussion: none of the 1000 rows carried price or currency filters — the two shapes that exceeded the 150ms timeout in the 7/24 filter-combination probe — and real pCIV traffic will send price ranges. In the deployed schema, price, salePrice, and currencyCode all lack fast-search.
